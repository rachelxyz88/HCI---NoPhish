Phishing Scam Using QR Code

Educational Goals

Users understand that phishing doesn't only happen through suspicious emails or strange links. Today, scammers also use QR codes that look clean, official, and trustworthy — often placed on posters, messages, or service notifications.

What is a QR Code Phishing Scam?

Users understand that QR Code Phishing (also called Quishing) is a scam technique where attackers create fake QR codes that direct victims to malicious websites or steal personal information.

  • Uses logos and designs similar to official emails
  • Uses similar email domains (e.g., @tokoped1a-support.com)
  • Contains urgent messages like “Your account will be blocked in 24 hours!”
  • Fake CTA buttons/links like “Verify Now”

"Fake Emails Using BRI Bank Name Harm Many Customers"

Victims clicked links in fake emails that appeared to be from banks, redirected to fake websites, then drained their funds within minutes.

Why Do So Many People Get Fooled?

  • The QR code looks professional and legitimate
  • Victims feel safe because QR codes are seen as convenient and harmless
  • “If this is fake, why does it look real?”
  • Urgent messages cause panic
  • People rarely check email address details
The Mindset That Users Should Cultivate: Don’t trust a QR scan automatically, always check the domain shown after scanning and never enter login credentials on a page you didn’t navigate to intentionally
